# Lens 09 — Information Architecture & Homepage Structure

## VERDICT (3 sentences max)
The homepage has no spine: it is **10 near-identical rails with no hierarchy**, so the eye lands nowhere and the visitor never learns in 5 seconds that BollyAI is an *answer engine* rather than another listicle blog. Collapse 10 sections to **6**, give the page ONE job above the fold (a single commanding "Friday verdict" billboard), and make **ask/search the load-bearing element of the chrome** because the product's promise is "Har Friday ka faisla," not "browse our rails." Then descend in strict priority — verdict → this week → box-office board → desks → discovery — so every scroll answers a sharper question than the last.

## WHAT THE BEST DO  (named examples + the MECHANISM — why it works)
- **One commanding above-the-fold "stage," not a grid** — *Perplexity*, *Apple product pages*, *The Athletic match pages*. NN/g's finding that users spend ~80% of attention above the fold means the fold must carry a SINGLE focal object with one job. A bento wall of 9 equal tiles forfeits this: nine focal points = zero focal points. The mechanism is **pre-attentive salience** — the eye locks onto the largest high-contrast object; give it exactly one.
- **Search/ask is the centerpiece for an answer product** — *Perplexity* opens with the query box dead-center and sources-first answers; the entire chrome IS the ask box. For a product whose tagline is a *verdict promise*, burying "ask" in an 11-item top strip contradicts the value prop. Mechanism: the homepage's hero element should BE the core action (Jakob's "the interface should reflect the product's primary verb" — here the verb is *ask/decide*, not *browse*).
- **The "≤7 top-level items" rule** — IA canon (Miller-adjacent, codified by NN/g and every 2026 IA guide). BollyAI's live nav is **11 items** (7 desks + Box Office + Series + What to Watch + OTT Calendar). The fix the best sites use: **primary nav = 4-5 destinations; everything else folds into a mega-menu or a single "Desks" group.** Mechanism: working-memory ceiling — past ~7 choices, scan-time and abandonment rise.
- **Finite, purposeful rails with descending specificity** — *The Athletic*, *Letterboxd*, *Apple TV editorial*. Each rail answers a DIFFERENT question (newest / hottest / where-to-watch), and the page reads like a narrowing funnel, not a flat stack. Baymard + the Netflix decision-fatigue case study both show that **rails that feel identical induce fatigue and scroll-blindness** — users stop registering them as distinct. Mechanism: **information scent** — each header must promise a clearly different reward.
- **A live, earned "board" as proof-of-authority** — *The Athletic* live tables, *trading dashboards*, *ESPN scoreboards*. A box-office leaderboard that updates and cites sources signals "this site KNOWS, right now." For a brand whose moat is *trust* (no fake ratings), a real-data board is the credibility engine social-proof counts would normally do. Mechanism: **competence signaling via live data** beats fake "10M reviews" badges — and it's the only honest lever available under BollyAI's fences.
- **Editorial "shelf" curation over algorithmic firehose** — *Criterion Channel*, *Apple TV "Great Watches"*, *Mubi*. A small number of human-curated shelves ("What to watch this weekend") out-converts twelve auto-generated rails because **a curated set implies a curator with taste** — the exact brand BollyAI sells (the disclosed critic who "read everyone who watched").
- **Wayfinding eyebrows + a one-line identity statement** — *Stripe*, *Linear*, *The Pudding*. A persistent eyebrow ("BollyAI · Friday verdict engine") + section kickers tell the visitor *where they are* and *what kind of place this is* within one glance. Mechanism: **orientation reduces cognitive load** before content load even begins.
- **Strict mobile priority-stacking, not a desktop reflow** — *The Athletic*, *Perplexity* mobile. The best mobile homepages don't shrink the desktop; they **re-order by job**: identity → ask → the one verdict → this week → board. Mechanism: on a 1-column phone, *vertical order IS the hierarchy* — there is no "right rail" to demote things into.

## WHERE BOLLYAI FAILS TODAY  (grounded in screenshot / live DOM / code)
- **The hero is a bento, not a stage.** Screenshot shows "Karuppu" lead tile fighting a wall of 8 secondary tiles (Game of Thrones, Dridishyam 2, Thodra Ke More Pyaar, Pddtl, Hai Zaaaai..., Mollywood Times, Bouf, Touch You a Lemon) at near-equal weight. The eye bounces; nothing says "START HERE." The single most valuable pixel-real-estate is spent on a grid.
- **Nav is overloaded — 11 items, confirmed live** (Bollywood/Kollywood/Tollywood/Mollywood/Sandalwood/Hollywood/Streaming/Box Office/Series/What to Watch/OTT Calendar). It's a thin strip carrying a sitemap. Past ~7 it stops being navigation and becomes noise; on mobile this is unusable as a bar.
- **No "answer engine" signal in 5 seconds.** The page reads as a magazine of rails. The product's entire identity — *ask a movie, get the verdict* — is invisible above the fold. There is no ask box, no "Is it worth watching?" prompt, no verdict-ladder explainer. A first-time visitor cannot tell this is an answer engine vs. a Bollywood news blog.
- **10 rails = decision fatigue + scroll-blindness.** Sections 3,5,7,8 (Just Dropped / Big This Week / Naye Episode Reviews / What to Watch) are all the **same horizontal MediaCard rail** with different headers. The Netflix case study + Baymard both flag this: identical-feeling rails make users stop reading headers and disengage. Four of the ten sections are visually interchangeable.
- **Hierarchy is flat — every section is "medium."** No section is clearly the most important; the trade-ticker (section 2) and the desk-strip (section 10) get similar visual investment as the actual verdict content. There is no descending priority; it's a stack of equals.
- **Redundant desk navigation appears THREE times** — top nav desks (1), "Desk-nav" 6 tiles (section 4), and "Desk-strip" 6 tiles (section 10), plus "2026 Yearboards" 7 desk links (section 9). That's the same wayfinding job done four times. Pure duplication = wasted scroll + diluted scent.
- **Box Office + OTT board is buried at section 6**, below two media rails — yet a live, cited box-office board is BollyAI's single strongest *honest* authority signal. The most credibility-dense, most-honest element is in the middle of the page where it does the least work.
- **The ticker (section 2) is decoration, not navigation.** A scrolling marquee of figures directly under a bento adds motion-noise and a second "data" zone competing with the eventual board, without giving the user a decision or a destination.

## TOP 5 CONCRETE CHANGES  (ranked; each implementable)
1. **Replace the bento `FeaturedMosaic` with a single full-bleed "Verdict Billboard" (`<VerdictStage>`).** One backdrop at **68–74vh**, gradient scrim `linear-gradient(180deg, transparent 0%, oklch(8% .025 280 / .15) 35%, oklch(8% .025 280 / .92) 100%)`. Content block bottom-left: desk eyebrow ("KOLLYWOOD · FRIDAY VERDICT") → Fraunces title at `clamp(3.2rem, 8vw, 7.2rem)` → one verdict line ("Tracking disaster — Rs 304cr WW but…") → a **BollyMeter chip top-left** (e.g. `4.1/10`) → ONE primary CTA ("Read the verdict →") + one ghost CTA ("Where to watch"). Kill the live-stats ribbon here; move counts to the board (#3) and footer. **The 8 secondary tiles become section #2.** This gives the fold exactly one job.
2. **Make ASK the centerpiece of the hero, not a nav afterthought.** Float a persistent **ask bar pinned to the bottom of the billboard** (or immediately under it on mobile): full-width pill, placeholder *"Is it worth watching? Ask BollyAI…"*, with 3 tap-chips below it (`Box office?` · `Where to stream?` · `Worth it?`). Client-only: route to a static `/ask?q=` results page or scroll-to-search; **no backend needed** (build-time index of titles + client fuzzy match over the existing JSON catalog). This is the move that finally *announces* "answer engine" in 5 seconds — the verb of the product becomes the first interactive object.
3. **Cut the top nav from 11 → 5 items + a "Desks" mega-menu; promote search.** Primary bar: **`BollyAI` logo · Box Office · This Week · Desks ▾ · [persistent search icon→expands]`**. "Desks ▾" is a mega-menu listing all 7 with live title-counts + accent dots (replacing both section-4 desk-nav AND section-10 desk-strip). "Series / What to Watch / OTT Calendar" move under a slim secondary row or into the mega-menu's right column. On mobile: hamburger → **drawer** with search at top, then the 5 primaries, then desks accordion. Net: chrome goes from "sitemap strip" to "premium 5-item bar with a strong search affordance."
4. **Re-order to a 6-section descending-priority spine** (replaces the 10-section stack):
   - **§1 Verdict Billboard** — the single Friday faisla (job: *land the eye, state identity, offer ask*).
   - **§2 "Big This Friday" — a 1-lead + 4-secondary editorial block** (NOT a rail; absorbs old bento's 8 tiles + Just Dropped's freshest). Asymmetric grid: one wide tile + four standard. Job: *the week's other verdicts, ranked, scannable in one screen — no horizontal scroll.*
   - **§3 The Board — `<LiveBoard>` box-office + OTT, side by side, full-bleed dark panel** (promote section 6 up; fold the section-2 ticker INTO this board as its top strip). Job: *the live, cited proof BollyAI knows right now* — the honest authority engine. Counts (559 series / 62 films / freshness) live as the board's caption.
   - **§4 Desks — one mega-row of 7 desk tiles with accent tint + count + "latest verdict" line** (merges old sections 4, 9, 10 into ONE). Job: *pick your cinema* — the single wayfinding shelf.
   - **§5 "What to Watch" — 2-3 curated editorial shelves** (absorbs old §7 episode reviews + §8 watchlists into curated, human-titled shelves, max 3, each a DIFFERENT question: "Best this weekend" / "New episodes worth your time" / "Hidden on OTT"). Job: *editorial discovery with taste.*
   - **§6 Footer-band** — identity statement, the AI-disclosure tagline, /takedown, desks, freshness. Job: *trust + close.*
   This is a funnel: verdict → week → live truth → choose desk → curated discovery → trust. Each step a sharper question.
5. **Add a persistent identity eyebrow + section kickers for scent.** A thin top-of-page strip (above or within nav): **"BollyAI · Har Friday ka faisla · AI critic that has read everyone who watched."** Every section gets a consistent eyebrow kicker in JetBrains Mono caps (`§ THE BOARD`, `§ THIS FRIDAY`, `§ PICK A DESK`) so a visitor always knows where they are and that the place has a single editorial voice. Tiny cost, large orientation payoff.

## LATEST TECH / UI NOTE  (2026 primitive within static-export + ₹0 fence)
- **CSS `position: sticky` + `scroll-driven animations`** (`animation-timeline: view()` / `scroll()`) — now baseline in 2026 evergreen browsers, **zero JS, zero library** (fits the no-framer-motion fence). Use for: the billboard scrim deepening as you scroll past it, section eyebrows fading in on entry, the live-board strip pinning briefly. Gives the "latest UI" motion language the brief asks for *without* adding tooling.
- **Cross-document View Transitions API** (`@view-transition { navigation: auto }`) — a single CSS line in `globals.css` gives smooth poster/title morph between the homepage and a verdict page on a **static multi-page export**. This is the headline "feels like an app" win that's fully compatible with `output: export` (no SSR). Tag the billboard poster + title with `view-transition-name` so they animate into the article hero.
- **Client-only ask index** — build-time generate a `titles-index.json` (slug + title + aliases + desk) from the existing series/film JSON; ship a ~3KB fuzzy matcher (e.g. uFuzzy) so the hero ask bar resolves instantly client-side. No server, no DB — pure static, satisfies "answer engine" UX honestly.
- **`content-visibility: auto`** on every below-fold section — free rendering-perf win on mobile (the dominant traffic), lets the heavy poster grids skip layout/paint until scrolled near. Pairs perfectly with cutting to 6 sections.

## ANTI-SLOP WARNING  (the generic redesign trap for this lens)
- **Do NOT just "add another hero carousel."** Swapping the bento for an auto-rotating slideshow is the #1 cliché and is *worse* — Baymard's data shows hero carousels get ignored and the 2nd+ slides are near-invisible. The fix is a **single static stage**, not a rotator.
- **Do NOT keep 10 rails and merely restyle them.** If the output is "same 10 sections but prettier cards," the structural failure ("the whole structure doesn't look good") is untouched. The win is **fewer, differentiated** sections — refuse to ship more than ~6.
- **Do NOT bolt a fake search bar that just filters a rail.** An answer-engine ask box that doesn't actually answer is slop and breaks the brand-trust promise. It must resolve to a real verdict/where-to-watch destination (even if static).
- **Do NOT triplicate desk navigation "for SEO."** One strong desk shelf + a mega-menu covers crawl + UX. Four desk grids is the current disease, not a feature.
- **Do NOT pile grain/scanline texture on the billboard to "look cinematic."** On a large hero with possibly low-res harvested press stills, texture overlays read as muddy, not filmic. Keep the stage clean; reserve texture for small surface panels (the board), per Lens-on-art-direction.

## ONE BIG SWING  (the "kamaal" move only this lens sees)
**Make the homepage itself the answer engine — a "Friday Faisla" command surface, not a magazine.** Above the fold: the single biggest verdict of the week as a full-bleed billboard, and **directly fused to it, the ask bar IS the hero's base** — type any title, get its BollyMeter + where-to-watch + one-line faisla *inline, client-side, instantly* (static index, no backend). The page literally demonstrates the product in the first screen: ask → verdict, the exact loop the tagline promises. Below that, a **single live "Friday Board"** (box office + OTT, cited, ticking) as the honest-authority proof, then desks, then curated shelves. The radical reframe: BollyAI's homepage stops imitating a streaming-service rail-wall (a category it can't out-poster, given incomplete art) and becomes the one thing none of the big sites are — **a decision instrument you operate.** That's the moat: when posters are incomplete and you can't fake social proof, *interaction + cited live truth* is the differentiated authority. Lead with the verb. Make them ask. Give the faisla.
